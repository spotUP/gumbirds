# Hazards v4: depth pass on v3

Drafts only. Generated by `draw_hazards_v4.py` (`python3.14 -I draw_hazards_v4.py`, options `--only <keys>` and
`--check <dir>` for inspection sheets outside this directory). Open `index.html` to compare v4 with v3.

Motion and physics models are those of v3. Changes are shading, palettes, tile variants and the placement of
sources. Two model parameters moved: lava crust speed (7 to 10 px per loop, for longer plates) and the fire
cooling term (weaker sources sit in colder air, so their tongues stay short).

## Light source

One key light from the upper left on all four hazards. Lit faces look up and left, shadow faces look down and
right, cast shadows fall to the lower right. Flame and melt are emissive: they light coal rims, crust undersides
and crack walls from below. Cast light on the scene is the v3 code, unchanged; fire and lava now give 8 light
columns per 128 px (4 per tile variant).

## Result per point

1. Depth, all four: done. Hard cel clusters, no ramps, no ordered dither, no speckle.
   - Spikes: steel in 7 shades (outline, core shadow, shadow, mid, light, highlight, white). Blade has a lit left
     face, white rim, ridge glint, core shadow behind the ridge, reflected light on the far edge and a form shadow
     wedge at the collar. Shaft is a chrome column with a cast shadow under the collar. Each sleeve is a bevelled
     block: top face, front face with a diagonal shadow, violet bounce light on the right edge. The arc puts a
     cyan rim on the two blade faces next to it.
   - Fire: white core, yellow, amber, orange, red, crimson, cool magenta edge; a darker back row of flames;
     two violet smoke shapes above the tips, made from a soot field that the same gas flow carries. Coal lumps have
     an ash-lit top left, mid, dark and outline clusters, and a rim that glows with the heat above it.
   - Water: lit crest, cyan skin, teal band where light enters the wave, light blue band, indigo self-shadow
     behind each crest, blue, indigo and navy depth bands, caustic shapes one shade lighter.
   - Lava: melt in 7 shades (violet deep shadow to white-hot). Crust plates have a top face (highlight at the end
     that faces the light), side face, violet shadow end, black underside, a glow row under the plate and a cast
     shadow on the melt (two rows, one shade darker). Cracks are shallow notches filled with glow; crack walls glow.
     Bubble domes have a white rim to the light, a yellow body and an orange, red and dark red far side.
2. Lava bubble repeat: fixed with a second tile variant. The lava is simulated on a 128 px periodic domain and cut
   into variant A (x 0-63) and B (x 64-127). The big bubble (radius 7.5) is in B only: 2 times across 320 px.
   Three smaller bubbles (radius 4.6, 3.4, 3.0) burst at other frames. Five blisters at uneven places.
3. Fire tongues: fixed with a second tile variant, same method. Nine sources, strengths 0.38 to 1.00, gaps
   12 18 10 19 17 9 17 11 15 px. The script prints the tallest tongues per frame. The two tallest are within
   1 px of each other in 4 of 16 frames (frames 4, 7, 13, 15; v3: about a third), so the height point is only
   partly fixed. In those frames the two tongues stand at uneven distances, so they do not read as a pair.
4. Water rocks and weed: fixed. Rocks use three violet shades (shadow face, lit face, highlight edge) and weed two
   greens (lit, shadow), all far from the outline colour. Stalks are 2 px wide with leaves. Readable at 1x.

## Frame time and tile cost

| Hazard | Video frames per frame | Loop | Variants | Tiles (16x16) | Unique tiles |
|--------|------------------------|------|----------|---------------|--------------|
| Spikes | 4 | 64 | 1 | 128 | 47 |
| Fire | 4 | 64 | 2 (A, B) | 256 | 254 |
| Water | 6 | 96 | 1 | 128 (plus lurker sprite) | 99 |
| Lava | 8 | 128 | 2 (A, B) | 256 | 256 |

The strip is A B A B A. A and B are two halves of one 128 px periodic image, so every joint (A to B, B to A) is
seamless in every frame. All loops are exactly periodic in time (the script asserts this for the simulations).

## Files

- `frames_<K>_1x.png`: build input. 16 frames left to right, 64x32 each. Row 0 is variant A, row 1 is variant B
  (fire and lava only). Colours map to the palette index order above.
- `scene_<K>_3x.png`: full screen, frame 2, 3x.
- `anim_<K>_16f_4x.gif`: 320 px strip plus 56 px of scene, 4x, real frame timing (rounded to 10 ms).

## Still weak

- Fire: frame 2 (the still) has only one tall tongue per 128 px; the fire is lower on average than v3.
  Smoke shapes are large flat blobs at 1x.
- Lava: plates are short (4 to 8 px), so the crust still reads busy at 1x. Tile cost doubled, no tile reuse.
- Spikes: the palette is full (15). The gain is smaller than on the other three because v3 already had 5 steel shades.
- Water: weed green is strong against the blue; lower it if it pulls the eye from the lurker.
- Cast light is checked against the draft scene only, as in v3.
- Tile cost of fire and lava is 256 each. If that is too much, drop the back row of flames or halve the frame count of variant B.
